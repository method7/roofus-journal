## In plain English

My leads agent keeps a business on file only once Companies House (the
government's register of companies) or the business's own website
confirms who it is (decision 0018). Sole traders and partnerships aren't
on Companies House, so until now none of them could be kept. Now Simon
reads a business's website and types its name and town into a short
command, with the page's address.

Simon also wanted two professional registers to confirm a business. When
one of my agents read their terms, one forbids it and the other can't
find a business by its name and town. So neither does.

Letters and emails now use the name a business gives on its own website.
Companies House writes most names in capitals, so without that name a
draft keeps the capitals and tells Simon to check.

## Context

Decision 0018 says a record's name, town and company number come from
Companies House or the business's own website, never from Google. Google
gives my agent only a list of places to look up. The confirm step looks
each one up on Companies House and keeps it only when exactly one company
matches its name and town. That left three gaps, which Simon agreed to
fill on 14 November 2026:

1. **Sole traders and partnerships.** Companies House doesn't list them,
   so every one was marked "not confirmed". My records store could already
   hold a business confirmed from its own website, but nothing let Simon
   do it.
2. **Clinics.** Osteopaths and physiotherapists are often sole traders.
   Each profession has a register: the General Osteopathic Council (GOsC)
   keeps one for osteopaths and the Health and Care Professions Council
   (HCPC) keeps one for physiotherapists. My business check already
   searches the GOsC register for a practice's contact details.
3. **Names in capitals.** Companies House writes "ZEBRA ROOFING LIMITED",
   so a letter opened "Dear ZEBRA ROOFING LIMITED".

**The registers' terms.** Before building the second part, one of my
agents read what each register says about using what it shows, on
14 November 2026.

- The GOsC's
  [data protection and privacy page](https://www.osteopathy.org.uk/about-gosc/corporate-information/data-protection-and-privacy/)
  (last updated 26 March 2026) says, under "The Register of Osteopaths":
  "Under the provisions of the Data Protection Act 2018, the reproduction
  and use of names and addresses on this website for commercial purposes
  is strictly prohibited." Keeping a practice's name and town to write to
  it about Method7's work is a commercial purpose.
- The HCPC has no separate terms of use. Its
  [policies page](https://www.hcpc-uk.org/about-us/corporate-governance/policies/)
  says the terms for its website are in its
  [privacy notice](https://www.hcpc-uk.org/about-us/corporate-governance/policies/privacy-notice/)
  (last reviewed 5 August 2026). That lists what the register shows for
  each person: name, registration number, location, status and period of
  registration. It then says: "This information may be used by third
  parties in the provision of their services to validate persons employed
  as registrants of the HCPC." That allows checking a person someone
  employs. It says nothing about keeping a business's details. The
  [register search](https://www.hcpc-uk.org/check-the-register/) also
  finds people, not businesses: it asks for a surname or registration
  number and a profession, through a form that needs a security code from
  the page. It can't be searched by a business's name and town at all.

## Options

For sole traders:

1. **Leave them out.** No record unless Companies House confirms it.
2. **Let Simon confirm one by hand from its own website.** Simon reads
   the website. My agent takes what Simon types and never reads the site
   itself.

For the registers:

1. **Confirm from the GOsC and HCPC registers** on one exact match of
   name and town, as Companies House does now.
2. **Don't use them to confirm a business.**

For names:

1. **Change the capitals**, so "ZEBRA ROOFING LIMITED" becomes "Zebra
   Roofing Limited". That is a guess. Some guesses are wrong: "SP
   ROOFING LTD" would become "Sp Roofing Ltd".
2. **Keep the name a business uses on its own website**, as Simon types
   it. Use it in letters and emails. Without one, keep Companies House's
   name exactly and tell Simon.

## Decision

Simon chose option 2 for sole traders and option 2 for names on
14 November 2026. Simon chose option 1 for the registers. Before building
it, one of my agents read their terms, as the task asked. One forbids it
and the other can't find a business, so that part was not built: neither
register confirms a business. One of my agents built the rest:

- **Confirming by hand.** Simon gives the place ID, the name and town as
  the website gives them and the page's address. The record's source is
  the business's own website, with that address and the day. The business
  check then runs on what Simon typed, as for any other record. A place
  ID already confirmed, or linked to a record, is refused. So is an
  address that isn't a web page's (one starting http:// or https://).
- **The business's own name.** Simon can add the name a business uses on
  its own website to any record, with the page's address as its source.
  Drafts greet and address the business by that name. The name on file
  from Companies House stays as it is, because it is how the record is
  matched.
- **Capitals left alone.** Without that name, a draft uses Companies
  House's name exactly as it is written. If it is all in capitals, the
  draft carries a note for Simon: "This name is as Companies House writes
  it. Check how the business writes its own name before sending." No
  name's capitals are ever changed by guesswork.

## Consequences

- Sole traders and partnerships can now go on file, but only one at a
  time, by Simon. The confirm step still marks them "not confirmed"
  first.
- Confirming a business by hand takes Simon's time: reading the website,
  then typing what it says. A typing mistake goes into the record.
- Clinics that are sole traders get no help from the registers. They go
  on file only when Simon confirms them from their own website.
- Every letter to a company whose name Companies House writes in capitals
  waits for Simon to check the name, unless Simon has already added the
  business's own name.
- The GOsC line affects more than this choice. My business check already
  reads the GOsC register for a practice's principal (the osteopath who
  runs it), its address and its contact details. It stores those with the
  check and can name the principal in a letter. That is also using names
  and addresses from the register for a commercial purpose. Simon has not
  decided what to do about it yet. Nothing in this change alters it.
- No lawyer has looked at either register's terms.

Update, 14 November 2026: Simon decided the GOsC question. My business
check no longer reads the GOsC register at all. A letter is never
addressed to a principal named on it. Decision 0020 explains why and what
it means for clinics.

Update, 15 November 2026: decision 0021 replaces the "confirming by hand"
part of this decision. My leads agent now reads a business's own website
and Simon confirms or rejects what it read with one key. Confirming by
hand stays only for a business with no website, or a site my agent can't
read. The rest of this decision still stands: letters and emails use the
name a business gives on its own website and no name's capitals are
changed by guesswork.
