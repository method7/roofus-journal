## In plain English

Until now my leads agent wrote two kinds of letter: the Founding Ten offer
for trades and clinics and an introduction to Method7's software work for
firms with paperwork to manage. Neither suits a food bank or a village
hall. Simon chose a third letter, for charities, clubs and community
groups, with special rates. Simon wrote it.

## Context

Decision 0014 split the letters into two groups, each with its own
offer. Each kind of business my leads agent searches for, such as
roofers or accountants, belongs to one group, which decides the letter it
gets. Charities, sports clubs, churches and community groups fitted
neither. The Founding Ten is about winning customers. The software letter
is about a firm's records. A group run by volunteers has different
worries: bookings, members and keeping everyone informed, with little
time or money to spare.

On 17 November 2026 Simon asked for a group of its own for them and wrote
the letter. It names a club Method7 has built a website for, Moonrakers
Judo Club. It offers special rates for building a website, making one
easier to manage, hosting and ongoing support.

## Options

1. **Send them the Founding Ten letter.** It offers ten websites at half
   price to local businesses. A charity isn't a business looking for
   customers, so the letter would read oddly.
2. **Leave them out.** Simple, but Simon wants to do more for local
   groups.
3. **A third group with its own letter.** More to look after, but each
   reader gets a letter written for them.

## Decision

Simon chose option 3. The new group is called community. Its letter is
Simon's own words. The email is a shorter version made from Simon's
sentences, under the subject "Special rates for local charities and
community groups". In each, one of my agents writes one or two sentences
about the group and what it does, from the facts held about it and
nothing else. Those sentences never say or hint that the group needs
help or lacks anything. The check of facts reads them, as it reads every
draft. The line at the end that says where the details came from talks
about "your organisation's details", since a charity is not a business.

The Find page has a new list, Charities and community groups, with 20
kinds such as charities, village halls, sports clubs, churches, choirs and
food banks. Its letter starts set to community. Martial arts schools moved
there from Fitness and coaching. No list includes state schools.

The letter quotes no prices: "special rates" is as far as it goes, as
Simon wrote it.

## Consequences

- Charities, clubs and community groups now get a letter that fits them.
- Simon's letter is longer than the others: about 230 words with the
  greeting and the tailored sentences. The drafting check allowed under
  200, so a community letter now has a limit of 260. Emails keep the
  200 limit.
- Simon's letter calls the Moonrakers website "simple, fast". The check
  that stops a draft remarking on the reader's website, its speed
  included, now skips Simon's own fixed paragraphs, so that line passes.
  The same word in the sentences my agent writes still fails.
- A third group means a third set of wording to keep up to date: the
  letter, the email, the subject, the lines for a phone call and the
  privacy line.
- Simon chose this on 17 November 2026. It is proposed until Simon
  accepts it.
