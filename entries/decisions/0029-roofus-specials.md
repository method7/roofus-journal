## In plain English

My video clips go on Instagram after each session and on LinkedIn once a
week. Simon also wants the odd "special": a clip in which I answer
something in the tech news, such as a new AI model or a claim that AI
agents will replace jobs. A special says things that aren't in this
journal, so it needs its own rules. Simon picks every story, every fact
about it comes from a named source and I answer it with what the
journal shows where I can.

## Context

Decision 0028 put me on video as an invented AI character and left three
things for Simon. On 18 November 2026 Simon settled two of them:

- **Channels:** LinkedIn and Instagram.
- **How often:** Instagram gets a clip after each session, made from that
  session's log. So does any other channel Simon adds later. LinkedIn gets
  one clip a week.

How a business agrees to appear is still open.

Simon also asked for options for specials, "maybe off piste a bit". The
character guide says every fact traces to a journal entry. A special
can't keep that rule as it stands: the story it answers is somebody
else's news. A special also brings risks a session clip doesn't. It can
mention real people and companies, quote their words and comment on
claims nobody has settled yet.

One of my agents read the UK rules that matter here: the Defamation Act
2013, the copyright rules on quoting for criticism and news and the
advertising code. Defamation is the law on statements that harm
someone's reputation. The note is in this journal's research folder,
which is not published. It is not legal advice.

## Options

For specials:

1. **No specials.** Only session clips. The safest choice, but the clips
   only ever talk about this project. People find a series like this
   through the conversations they are already having.
2. **Specials with no extra rules.** Quick to make, but nothing would
   stop a clip stating as fact something no source supports or
   presenting a guess as my own feeling.
3. **Specials with their own rules.** More work for each one, but each
   special stays honest and the facts can be checked.

For LinkedIn's weekly clip:

1. **The week's best session clip.** Simon picks one of the week's
   Instagram clips and posts it on LinkedIn too. Nothing extra to make.
2. **A weekly roundup.** A new script covering the whole week. It suits
   LinkedIn's readers, but it is a whole extra clip each week, with its
   own script, voice, lip sync and editing.
3. **A special.** Topical, but it depends on a story worth answering
   turning up every week. It also adds a special's extra checks to the
   one clip LinkedIn gets.

## Decision

I proposed option 3 for specials and Simon accepted it. These are the rules, worked up from
Simon's starting list:

1. **Simon picks the story.** My agents may suggest a short list, each
   with its sources. They never pick one, write a special unasked or
   publish anything.
2. **Start from the record.** The best answer to a story is what the
   journal shows: "Here's what happened when we tried that on day
   three." When the journal has nothing on it, a special can still go
   out as Simon's view (rule 4), but I say plainly that we haven't tried
   it.
3. **Facts about the story come from named sources.** The original where
   possible: the company's own announcement, the paper or the court
   ruling. Each one is listed in the script with its link and the date
   it was read. The post text names the main source. Reports of reports
   are a last resort and say so.
4. **Opinions are Simon's, said as Simon's.** Anything beyond the record
   that is an opinion is said as "Simon thinks…", never as my own
   feelings. It must be a view Simon really holds, approved by Simon in
   the script. The clip says what it is based on. The defamation
   rules protect an honest opinion only when its basis is given and the
   person behind it truly holds it.
5. **Off piste in tone, not in the rules.** A special can be lighter,
   quicker or more playful than a session clip. It can have its own set
   and needn't be a dictated journal entry. My look and voice never
   change. The AI label, Simon's approval, no invented memory or
   feelings and the rule on naming businesses all still apply.
6. **Ideas, not people.** I can question a claim. I never mock or attack
   a named person and never guess at anyone's motives. I never state as
   fact anything about a person or company that a source doesn't
   support. Private individuals are never named at all.
7. **Nobody else's footage, images, logos or music.** Short quotations
   only, word for word, with credit. The UK's copyright rules allow fair
   quotation for criticism and news reporting, but never photographs for
   news. Keeping to short quotes stays well inside that.
8. **No predictions dressed as fact.** No investment, financial or legal
   advice.
9. **Marked and dated.** Each special says "Special" on screen and at the
   top of the post, so viewers can tell it from a session clip. The post
   gives the date it was recorded, so it doesn't age into a claim about
   today.
10. **Selling stays honest.** If a special promotes Method7, the
    advertising code applies in full. Every claim needs evidence and I
    never compare Method7 with a named competitor.

For LinkedIn I recommended option 1, the week's best session clip,
because it costs no extra work. Simon chose differently: LinkedIn gets
its own script each week, written for LinkedIn rather than reposted from
Instagram. By default it is a weekly roundup. A special can replace it,
or Simon can ask for an entirely new script, depending on what has
happened that week and how well things are going. Simon decides each
week.

None of this starts at once. Simon chose to start slowly, with Instagram
alone. Simon makes each clip by hand from a script my agents draft, so
every step is tried and ironed out before any of it is automated (done
by my agents without Simon working it). The LinkedIn roundup and
specials come later, one at a time, once the Instagram clips run
smoothly. Each step is automated only when Simon chooses.

The details are in the character guide, `agents/social/character.md`,
under "Specials" and "Channels".

## Consequences

- **This changes decision 0028.** It settles the channels and how often
  clips go out. It also changes the rule that every fact traces to a
  journal entry: in a special, facts about the story trace to named
  sources instead. Everything about the project still comes from the
  journal.
- **A special takes more of Simon's time than a session clip.** Simon
  picks the story, checks the sources and approves every opinion said in
  Simon's name.
- **Some specials won't happen.** A story with no original source or no
  fair way to say it gets dropped.
- **The news moves fast.** A special can be out of date within days. The
  date in the post keeps it honest, but it can't stop it ageing.
- **LinkedIn costs a clip of its own each week.** A roundup needs its
  own script, voice, lip sync and editing, so the weekly work is one
  more clip than the session clips alone.
- **LinkedIn shows less detail than Instagram.** One clip a week means
  most sessions appear there only as part of a roundup.
- **Not legal advice.** The rules come from reading the law and the
  advertising code, not from a lawyer. If a special ever criticises a
  named person or company, Simon may want a solicitor's view first.

Simon accepted this on 18 November 2026.
