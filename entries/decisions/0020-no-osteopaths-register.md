## In plain English

My leads agent used to look up osteopaths' clinics on the General
Osteopathic Council's register. It took each clinic's address, phone,
email and the name of the osteopath who runs it. The register's own terms
forbid using its names and addresses for commercial purposes. Writing to
a clinic about Method7's work is one. So Simon chose to stop. A clinic is
now treated like any other business.

## Context

Decision 0019 found the problem. When one of my agents read the
registers' terms on 14 November 2026, the General Osteopathic Council
(GOsC) said this on its
[data protection and privacy page](https://www.osteopathy.org.uk/about-gosc/corporate-information/data-protection-and-privacy/),
under "The Register of Osteopaths":

> Under the provisions of the Data Protection Act 2018, the reproduction
> and use of names and addresses on this website for commercial purposes
> is strictly prohibited.

That decision stopped the register being used to confirm a business. But
my business check (the step that asks whether a business is still trading
before anyone writes to it) did more with the register. For any business
with "osteo" in its name, or when asked to, it searched the register by
town. When one practice matched, it:

- took the practice's address as the address to write to
- kept the emails, phones and websites the register listed
- named the practice's principal (the osteopath who runs it) as the
  person to write to, if the practice listed exactly one
- counted the listing as proof the practice still trades

All of that is using the register's names and addresses for a commercial
purpose. Decision 0019 left it for Simon to decide.

## Options

1. **Keep using the register** for clinics' details, despite its terms.
2. **Stop using it.** Take a clinic's details from the same places as
   every other business: its own website or Checkatrade, by hand
   (decision 0015).

## Decision

Simon chose option 2 on 14 November 2026. One of my agents built it:

- **The register is never read.** The business check no longer searches
  it, for any name. Asking for it by name is refused before anything is
  looked up.
- **No principal is named.** A letter is never addressed to a person
  named on the register. A record that already holds one gets a letter
  addressed to the business instead, the same as for any name my agent
  can't trust.
- **Old records are cleaned.** Records checked before this change may
  still hold what the register showed. Simon runs one command to remove
  it. The first time it only says how many records it would change, without
  naming any. Nothing changes until Simon runs it again to confirm. Each
  record it changes gets a note in its history. If the register was the
  only sign a practice still trades, that practice goes back to "not
  clear", for Simon to look at. Any action my agent had chosen from that
  check is cleared, so it is chosen again without the register.

The other register, kept by the Health and Care Professions Council for
physiotherapists, was never searched: decision 0019 explains why. My
business check still only notes that it can't be searched.

## Consequences

- A clinic that is a sole trader or partnership no longer counts as
  trading just because it is on the register. It goes on Simon's list to
  check by hand, as any sole trader does. Simon confirms it from its own
  website (decision 0019).
- A clinic that is a company on Companies House (the government's
  register of companies) is judged on Companies House alone, as before.
  It loses the address, contact details and principal the register gave.
  So a letter goes to its registered office, which is often an
  accountant's, unless Simon finds a better address by hand. The one
  thing Simon loses for a closed company is a note saying the practice
  was still on the register, perhaps under a new business.
- Fewer letters to clinics will name a person. A company's only director
  can still be named, from Companies House.
- Simon has to run the clean-up command on the records file. My agents
  never touch Simon's records.
- No lawyer has looked at the register's terms.
