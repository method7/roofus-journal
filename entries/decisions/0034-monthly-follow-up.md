## In plain English

My leads agent helps Simon introduce Method7 to local businesses by
letter, email and phone. Until now it had no follow-up. A business Simon
phoned and that said "not interested" was back on the next day's call
sheet. Simon has chosen differently. Once a business has been contacted,
it is never contacted again the next day. It moves onto a monthly
follow-up instead: each month, every business that hasn't heard from
Simon for a month gets a short follow-up email or letter, unless it has
asked to stop. Simon reads and approves each one before it goes, as
with every first letter and email.

## Context

Simon's words, on 21 November 2026: "all prospects once contacted should
not be contacted the next day, once introduced they move onto the
followup package... this will be sent periodically say for now monthly
to all prospects that havent been contacted for a month and are not on
the do-not-contact list".

Three things in my agent didn't fit that:

- **The call sheet.** It put a business back on the sheet every day,
  unless it had replied, said "not now", asked to be called back or was
  marked do not contact. Decision 0033 kept a business that said "not
  interested" on the phone on the next day's sheet, as if it hadn't
  picked up.
- **The daily queue.** Decision 0030's daily list of emails left out a
  business that had been written to, but not one Simon had only phoned.
- **No follow-up.** Decision 0031 noted that a follow-up letter or email
  was still to be written. Until then a business that said "not now"
  could only be phoned or written to by hand once its day came.

The businesses written to before 21 November 2026 were told their
details would be kept for six months. Decision 0032 keeps them for two
years and Simon chose to tell them so in the next letter or email.

## Options

1. **Keep it as it is.** Nothing to build, but a business could hear
   from Simon two days running and the first letter would be the last.
2. **A fixed number of follow-ups, then stop.** A business would get two
   or three, then nothing. It is gentle, but it ends the conversation
   for a business that might be ready later.
3. **Never the next day, then once a month.** Every business contacted
   once waits a month, then gets a follow-up to approve. It goes on each
   month until the business replies, says "not now" or asks to stop.

## Decision

Simon chose option 3 on 21 November 2026.

- **What counts as contact.** A letter, an email, an email from the
  daily queue confirmed sent, a "not now" reply or a call where the
  business took the call. These are the contacts decision 0032 counts,
  except one: a call with no answer is not a contact here.
- **Never the next day.** Once a business has been contacted it leaves
  the call sheet and the daily queue. It gets no first letter or email.
  So it can't be contacted again the next day. A business that asked to
  be called back is still called on the day it asked for.
- **Due a month after the last contact.** A business is due its
  follow-up a month after its last contact, to the day. A month is a
  setting, so Simon can change it.
- **Who is left out.** A business marked do not contact, one that has
  replied (a conversation is under way) and one that said "not now" with
  its day still ahead. A "not now" business is due from the day Simon
  chose (decision 0031).
- **Approved like the first.** Each follow-up is an email, or a letter
  where the business may not be emailed. It goes through the same review
  and send steps as the first contact. Simon starts each month's
  follow-ups from the page. Nothing sends them by itself.
- **Sending one starts the next month.** A follow-up sent is a new
  contact, so the next one is due a month later.
- **Fixed wording.** The follow-up is the same short text for every
  business, so my agent writes none of it and makes no claim to check.
  The wording is a first draft. Simon hasn't approved it yet. Each
  draft says so until Simon does.
- **The privacy notice.** A business first contacted before 21 November
  2026 gets one more paragraph in its next follow-up: its details are
  now kept for two years after the last contact, with the link to
  Method7's privacy notice. It is said once only.
- **A business with no way to write to it.** One that may not be
  emailed and has no postal address can't get a follow-up. While it is
  due, it is back on the call sheet.

## Consequences

- No business hears from Simon two days running.
- Every business contacted once stays in touch every month until it
  replies or asks to stop, without Simon having to remember.
- The call sheet is shorter: it is for businesses not yet contacted.
  This changes part of decision 0033. A "not interested" call no longer
  keeps the business on the next day's sheet. It is a contact, so the
  business waits for its follow-up.
- Simon has more to approve each month as the number of businesses
  contacted grows. Each still needs Simon's yes.
- The follow-up wording needs Simon's approval before the first one
  goes.
- A follow-up Simon sets aside leaves the business due one, so it comes
  back the next time Simon drafts the follow-ups.
- The privacy notice on method7.co.uk must say two years before the
  first follow-up goes, as decision 0032 says.

Simon has not accepted this yet.
