## In plain English

When a business answers one of Method7's letters or emails, it is
saying one of three things. It wants to talk, it doesn't want anything
right now, or it doesn't want to hear from Method7 again. Until now my
leads agent only knew the first and the last. I propose a middle one:
"not now". A business that says it isn't looking at the moment is not
asking Simon to stop. My agent keeps it, leaves it alone and reminds
Simon to get back in touch on a day Simon picks. Only a business that
asks not to be contacted again, or one that should never be written to,
is marked do not contact.

## Context

On 19 November 2026 the first reply came in from the 32 businesses
Simon has written to. It was polite: the business wasn't looking to
update its website at the moment, but thanked Simon for thinking of it.
Simon saw it as the ideal chance to get back in touch later.

My leads agent couldn't record that. It had two ways to mark a reply:

- **Replied**, which means a conversation is under way. Nothing more is
  drafted, sent or called for a replied business, because Simon carries
  the conversation on.
- **Do not contact**, which can't be undone. Nothing is ever drafted,
  sent or called for it again.

Neither fits. Worse, the leads agent's guide said "a business that says
no is marked do not contact". That rule came from my follow-up research
and it was wrong. The letters and emails promise only this: "If you'd
rather not hear from me, just reply and I won't contact you again." Not
at the moment is not that. The session log for the evening of 17
November repeats the same mistake. Old entries aren't edited, so the
next session log will correct it.

Simon had already recorded this reply as an ordinary reply, before the
"not now" choice existed. So a reply recorded that way needs to be able
to change to "not now".

## Options

1. **Keep two choices.** Mark a "not now" business replied and let Simon
   remember to get back in touch. Nothing to build, but nothing reminds
   Simon either. A replied business never shows as due anything.
2. **Mark it do not contact.** Safe in one sense, but it throws away a
   business that was friendly and breaks no promise by hearing from
   Simon again. It also treats a polite "not now" as if it were "stop".
3. **A third choice, "not now", with a day to get back in touch.** The
   business stays on file and is left alone until that day. Then my
   agent shows it as due a follow-up.

## Decision

I propose option 3.

- **What counts as a refusal.** A business that asks not to hear from
  Simon again, in any words. That is the promise in every letter and
  email, so it is always honoured and can't be undone. A business that
  should never be written to at all, such as a supplier, is marked do
  not contact too.
- **What doesn't.** "Not now", "not at the moment", "maybe next year" and
  other replies like them. They close nothing.
- **Simon picks the day.** Each time Simon records a "not now" reply,
  Simon chooses the day to get back in touch. The web page suggests
  about three months on, but Simon can pick any day still to come, up
  to six months after the reply.
- **Left alone until then.** Until that day nothing is drafted, sent or
  called for the business. Each step says why in one line, so it never
  looks like a mistake.
- **Due a follow-up from then.** From that day the overview, the replies
  list and the call sheet show it again.
- **Still open.** If the business later wants to talk, Simon marks it
  replied. If it later asks Simon to stop, Simon marks it do not contact.
- **A reply already recorded can change.** A reply recorded as an
  ordinary one can be changed to "not now". Its history keeps both
  entries, so nothing is lost.
- **Simon still reads every reply.** My agent never reads Simon's mailbox
  and never decides what a reply means. Simon decides and records it.

## Consequences

- The business that replied on 19 November can be kept for a follow-up
  instead of being lost.
- A follow-up letter or email is still to be written. Until it is,
  drafting and sending leave a "not now" business out even after its
  day. Simon can phone it, or write by hand.
- The six-month clean-up, which deletes a business that hasn't replied
  six months after the last contact, counts the "not now" reply as the
  last contact. So the day to get back in touch can be no more than six
  months after the reply. That clean-up isn't running yet (issue #113),
  so it is noted where that work will find it.
- One more thing for Simon to judge with each reply: is this "stop" or
  "not now"? When it isn't clear, do not contact is the safe answer.
- Do not contact still can't be undone. That hasn't changed.

Simon accepted this on 21 November 2026.

Update, 21 November 2026: decision 0034 changes what happens once a
"not now" business's day comes. It joins the monthly follow-up and gets
a follow-up letter or email for Simon to approve, rather than going back
on the call sheet. A business that can only be phoned still goes back on
the call sheet. The rest of this decision stands.
