This log starts on 15 November, where the last one stopped: one of my
agents was building the step that reads a business's own website.

## What we did

- **Ran the first real searches.** Simon ran the finding step on the
  roofers around Salisbury. It found 51 businesses worth a closer look.
  Each run showed something to fix, so most of these two days went on
  that.
- **Searched each area properly.** Google sends at most 20 places a page,
  so the step now reads every page. It searches inside each postcode
  district's own boundary, using Ordnance Survey's free postcode data. A
  search that finished in the last 90 days is skipped, so Simon doesn't
  pay twice for the same answer. A full run now covers all 15 districts in
  24 paid requests.
- **Kept builders, carpenters and scaffolders.** They turned up beside the
  roofers. Simon chose to count them as prospects too. Builders' merchants
  and other suppliers are now left out, by the kind of place Google says
  they are and a list of national chains.
- **Read company numbers from websites.** Two businesses waiting for Simon to check by hand had their company number on their own site. Now the agent reads
  it and checks it with Companies House itself. It also looks past the first page of Companies House results for a company with the same name
  (decision 0022).
- **Allowed letters to sole traders confirmed from their website.** A sole trader
  confirmed from its own site can now get a letter, but never an email
  (decision 0023).
- **Found where businesses actually trade.** A company's registered
  office is often its accountant's. Simon pointed out that the address on
  the business's own website is a better guide. The agent now reads the
  trading address from the site and won't write to a registered office it
  thinks is an accountant's (decision 0024).
- **Stopped Simon typing.** I planned 26 commands for Simon to type in
  addresses by hand. Simon said no: the agent should find them. Now a
  harvest step reads each business's site, proposes what it found and
  Simon presses y or n.
- **Added phone calls.** Some trades give a phone number and no address.
  Simon asked for a third way to reach them, beside the letter and the
  email (decision 0025). The agent makes a call sheet and keeps what each
  call led to. Simon tried the website of the Telephone Preference Service (TPS), the register of numbers that don't want sales calls. It only allows about five checks. Simon chose to
  wait and see how many calls there are before paying for a checking
  service.
- **Updated the privacy notice on method7.co.uk.** It now covers phone
  calls, the call log and Google as the way businesses are found.
- **Drafted every waiting business in one run.** Drafting used to need
  Simon to type the service and the customers for each business. Now one
  command drafts them all, reads the director's name from Companies House when a company has only one and adds a line saying where the details came from, with a link
  to the privacy notice. Simon reviews each draft with one key.
- **Built the send pack.** Accepted letters go on one printable page each,
  with the address placed for a window envelope. Accepted emails open in
  Mail, ready for Simon to read and send. Nothing sends by itself.
- **One message each, email first.** Simon decided each business hears from Method7
  once to start with: by email where that's allowed, otherwise by letter.
  Not both.
- **Rewrote the letters in Simon's words.** Simon didn't like the letter.
  Simon rewrote it, then wrote a shorter email, a new letter for businesses that might want software and
  a new note for a website with a fault. I checked each against the drafting rules (under 200 words, no comma before "and", no mention of speed) and suggested small changes. Simon chose the final wording, including
  the email subject: "Founding Ten: ten local websites at half price".

By the end of 16 November, 31 businesses had drafts: 14 emails and 17
letters. Nothing has been sent.

## What went wrong

- **I planned for Simon to type.** The website already had the addresses.
  Simon had asked for addresses to be stored and I didn't carry that
  through. Simon was right to be cross.
- **I said the automatic checks didn't run the leads agent's tests.** They do.
  Several of my agents then said the same thing in their reports. I
  corrected it each time.
- **Typed addresses were labelled as coming from the website.** When the harvest
  couldn't find an address, Simon typed one from Companies House. It was
  saved as if the website gave it, so a letter would have said "I found
  your details on your website". Simon removed them. The harvest now
  refuses an address whose postcode isn't on the site.
- **No emails were drafted.** The email addresses read from websites were
  stored, but the step that chooses email or post never saw them. Every
  company got a letter. Then the first fix only worked for a few records,
  because most companies' emails were never saved at all. Review caught
  that before Simon merged.
- **The send pack found no email addresses either.** It looked them up
  its own way, missing the same fix. I reviewed the send pack without
  testing it on a business whose email came only from its website. Simon
  found it on the first real run. The fix is waiting for Simon.
- **Drafts went out of date.** Each wording change left drafts in the old
  wording, with nothing to stop Simon accepting them. The agent now holds
  them back and can draft them all again in one go.
- **A decision note gave Simon reasons Simon never gave.** Review caught
  it before merging.
- **I treated the TPS as a gate.** Simon asked for calls and I suggested
  researching the rules first. Simon said I wasn't the gatekeeper. The
  call sheet carries a reminder instead.

## What I learned

- **Store what you read.** If the agent has seen an address or an email,
  it should keep it, with where it came from. Asking Simon to type it
  again is a fault.
- **Two steps that answer the same question must share the answer.** The
  drafting step and the send pack each worked out the email address. They
  disagreed.
- **Test a change on the records it was for.** Each email fix passed its
  own tests and still failed on Simon's real records.
- **Simon's words beat mine.** The letter Simon wrote is better than the
  one I drafted. My job is to check it against the rules, not to rewrite
  it.
