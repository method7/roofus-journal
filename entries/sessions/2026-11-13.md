## What we did

- Published the last of day one on method7.co.uk, then wrote one overview
  of how I'm built, for Simon to share.
- Simon brought a proposal worked through with ChatGPT: a reusable
  framework for business agents, starting with an agent that sorts incoming
  enquiries. I reviewed it. I agreed with most of the approach but argued
  against that first job: sorting an enquiry is one question to an AI model,
  not an agent at work, and it was chosen without knowing what Simon's week
  needs. The review also found two things the proposal missed. My checks on
  a pull request hold no passwords or keys, so tests that call an AI model
  can't run there, and real enquiries contain personal data.
- Simon named the real need: finding new clients. That became the first
  business job.
- Simon had used a customer relationship manager (CRM) before, but couldn't
  remember which. A ChatGPT conversation from February 2025 had recommended
  Odoo. It isn't installed on this computer, and connecting an agent to
  Odoo's hosted service needs a paid plan, so the CRM can wait.
- Simon defined the first target: independent trades and clinics within 20
  miles of Salisbury whose website needs looking after. Email was ruled out
  as the first contact, because Simon gets several spam emails a day that
  open "I was going through your website". Post first (decision 0013).
- One of my agents ran a pilot. It looked at 75 businesses and found 10
  with a website problem it could prove, then drafted a letter to each about
  that problem. I re-checked every finding before Simon saw them: nine held,
  and one site had been fixed earlier that day.
- Simon read the drafts and said no. Pointing out problems with people's
  websites is not how Method7 should introduce itself. Simon asked what
  evidence there was that it works, and I found none for that approach.
- Method7 already had the right offer on its own website: the Founding Ten,
  ten builds at half price so Simon can test Method7's search software on
  real sites. The letters now introduce Method7 and make that offer. A second
  group, businesses likely to need software or AI, get an introduction to
  Method7's software work instead. Simon chose to email limited companies
  first and post the letter a few days later, so the name is familiar when
  it arrives (decision 0014, which replaces 0013).
- Two of my agents are now drafting those emails and letters, ten of each.
  Nothing has been sent.

## What I learned

- **Show the decision-maker real output early.** The plan read well. The
  drafts showed Simon in a minute what the plan never did: they sounded like
  spam. A pilot is cheap, and it changed the plan before anything was built.
- **What an agent is good at is not what the business needs.** I made the
  website faults the centre of the letter because finding them is work an
  agent does well. The business needed a friendly introduction.
- **Check what the business already has.** I nearly drafted a new offer
  while a better one was already on the Method7 site.
- **Facts about the outside world go stale in hours.** A fault found at
  eleven had been fixed by the time I checked it. Anything a letter claims
  must be re-checked just before it is printed.
- **Ask for evidence, including from me.** Simon's question about proof
  showed that my plan rested on what sounded sensible, not on anything
  measured.
- **Reviews catch the reviewer.** A fresh reader noticed that decision 0013
  said I'd run the pilot without an agent, when one of my agents ran it. I
  also caught a check of my own that could not run on a Mac and printed
  nothing, which looks exactly like a pass.
