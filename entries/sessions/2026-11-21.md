This log carries on from 18 November, which ended with the first video
clip made and 32 businesses waiting to hear back. It covers the work
that merged on 20 November and early on 21 November.

## What we did

- **A second AI model joined.** Making the videos (see the log for
  18 November) took Simon into Google's AI tools. Simon then gave
  building work to Gemini, Google's AI model, through Antigravity,
  Google's AI code editor. Usually Claude builds everything here, so
  this is one place where the model matters.
- **Gemini built a daily outreach routine.** Simon wants a steadier way
  to find clients for Method7's software work across Wiltshire and
  Swindon: up to 20 emails each weekday, each one approved by Simon.
  Half go to the businesses my leads agent ranks highest and half to a
  deliberate mix of other kinds of business, so it learns which kinds
  respond. Gemini built it in stages that merged one after another on
  20 November:
  - settings files listing Method7's services and case studies, the
    kinds of business to aim at, how to score them and which AI model
    does each job
  - new places in the records for each business's profile, score, place
    in the day's queue and outcome
  - a way to find companies in Companies House's free monthly file
    (Companies House is the UK's official register of companies) and to
    read each one's website for signs it could use some software
  - the daily queue, the drafting and checking of each email, screens
    to approve each one and a report on what is working
  - a test that runs a whole made-up week from start to finish, plus a
    step-by-step guide for Simon's first real week.

  The plan behind it is decision 0030, still a proposal and not yet
  published.
- **Gemini ran out of hours.** On 20 November Gemini reached its
  five-hour usage limit twice. Simon's dashboard then showed 10% of the
  weekly allowance left, with 5 days and 13 hours until it reset.
  Gemini had built most of an import from the Companies House file onto
  the Find screen, but hadn't saved it. Simon chose to bring Claude in
  to finish the job rather than buy Gemini more credits. I saved
  Gemini's work as it was left, reviewed it and finished it.
- **Simon wrote about it.** Simon wrote a LinkedIn post about the
  handover, treating it as managing an AI workforce: who is available,
  how work is handed over and what the budget allows.
- **My agents then made the import and the page easier to use.**
  The import now shows its progress as it runs and reads the big
  monthly file only once. Every Companies House lookup is now paced, so
  no step goes over the limit Companies House sets. A double-click on
  Simon's Mac now starts the leads page, with no typing. A new Settings
  screen shows whether each key (the private code that lets the page
  use an online service) is set, without ever showing a key. It also
  backs up every record in one click.
- **The first reply said "not now".** A business replied that it
  wasn't looking at the moment, with thanks. Simon chose to treat that
  as a chance to get back in touch later, not a request to stop. My
  leads agent can now record a "not now" reply with a day to follow up,
  which Simon picks. Until then the business is left alone. Only a
  business that asks not to hear from Simon again is marked do not
  contact (decision 0031, proposed).
- **Simon chose to keep the research for two years.** My leads agent
  was to delete a business's record six months after the last contact.
  Simon rejected that: the research was paid for and is mostly about
  the business, not a person. Simon chose two years after the last
  contact. Only Simon can delete anything older: my leads agent lists
  exactly what would go and deletes it only when Simon types "y"
  (decision 0032, proposed).

That leaves 32 businesses written to, with one reply so far: a "not
now". The daily routine is built, with a guide for its first real
week.

## What went wrong

- **I gave Simon the wrong advice on the first reply.** I advised
  marking the business do not contact. Simon corrected me: "not at the
  moment" is not a request to stop, it is a chance to follow up later.
  That became the "not now" reply. The leads guide had the same mistake,
  saying a business that says no is marked do not contact. It is fixed.
- **An earlier log said it too.** The evening log for 17 November says
  "Anyone who says no is never contacted again". It should have said
  anyone who asks not to be contacted. Old entries aren't edited, so
  this is the correction.
- **The delete could have taken a current business's draft.** It first
  matched drafts by any mention of a business's name. A draft for a
  business being kept that mentioned one being deleted would have gone
  too. My review caught it. A kept business's draft is now never
  deleted, with a test that fails without the fix.
- **The first test of the delete proved only half of it.** It gave the
  delete its answer automatically, with no one typing it, so the delete
  refused and nothing was deleted. That showed only the refusal. The
  test was redone at a real terminal (the window where commands are
  typed by hand), where "n" and "yes" deleted nothing and "y" deleted
  only the record past its two years.
- **A missing key would have kept Simon off the page.** The agent that
  built the Mac launcher made it refuse to start if any key was missing.
  That would have locked Simon out of every screen over one key. My
  review made it a warning.
- **The Settings screen was cut off on a phone.** At phone width the
  column saying whether each key is set was off the edge of the table.
  My review moved it next to each key's name.
- **Nothing paced Companies House lookups.** Companies House allows 600
  lookups every five minutes and each business takes three. My review
  capped each import at 100 businesses to stay well inside that. The
  pacing came next. The cap is still 100 until Simon chooses to raise
  it.
- **A follow-up day could have fallen after the delete.** The first
  "not now" build let Simon pick a day after the six-month clean-up
  would have removed the record. My review made it refuse such a day.
  With two years now, the limit moved with it.

## What I learned

- **"Not now" is not "no".** Only a request to stop is a refusal. Ask
  Simon before closing a door.
- **Plan for an AI model's hours like a person's.** Usage limits and
  saved work matter when one model hands over to another.
- **Test the way it will really be used.** A delete that asks a
  question has to be tested at a real terminal, not by feeding it the
  answer.
- **A warning beats a lockout.** One missing key shouldn't stop every
  screen that doesn't need it.
