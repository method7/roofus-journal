This log carries on from the morning's entry, which ended with 31
businesses drafted and one fix waiting.

## What we did

- **Fixed the send pack.** It looked up each email address its own way
  and found none. Now the drafting step and the send pack ask the same
  question in the same place, so they can't disagree.
- **Emailed companies held back by an accountant's address.** Some
  companies were on the list for Simon to check by hand only because
  their registered office looked like an accountant's. A letter there
  might never reach them. Simon asked for those with an email on their
  own website to be emailed instead. One more business got an email this
  way. No letter goes to an address my leads agent doesn't trust.
- **Changed the contact details.** Simon asked for every email and letter
  to give the office landline and Simon's own email address, not the
  mobile and the general inbox. Drafts with the old details were held
  back and drafted again.
- **Sent the first emails.** Simon read each draft, opened the 15 emails
  in Mail and sent them. Each one is now recorded as sent, with the date.
- **Caught a fake email address.** One website gave two addresses: its
  real one and `info@email.com`, left over from the template the site
  was built with. My leads agent picked the fake one. I spotted it in the list the send pack printed. Simon changed it in Mail and sent the email to the real address. Now my leads agent throws
  away addresses like that and prefers one that matches the business's own website address.
- **Changed how this journal records work.** Not every change gets a
  decision record now. Simon asked for each session log to list what
  changed, taken from the work that was merged. That list is below.

The 17 letters are in the send pack, waiting for Simon to print and post
them.

## What changed

Merged on 16 November, in order:

1. Draft every waiting business in one run with nothing typed. Add the line saying where the details came from.
2. Fill in towns, refuse typed addresses the website doesn't give and
   find the company behind a website.
3. Email companies whose own website gives an email address.
4. Say "around Salisbury" in the opening of every letter and email.
5. Make a send pack of printable letters, open the emails in Mail and
   record what was sent.
6. Email first, with a letter only when there's no email.
7. Use Simon's own wording for every letter and email.
8. Send each email to the address chosen by the step that picks email or post.
9. Email companies held back only because of an accountant's address.
10. Give every email, then every letter, the landline and Simon's own
    address.
11. Never email a placeholder address. Prefer an email address that matches the business's own website.

## What went wrong

- **The send pack and the drafting step disagreed.** This is the lesson from the morning's log: two steps that answer the same question must share the answer. Simon's first real run showed it.
- **A fake address nearly went out.** My leads agent read two addresses from
  one site and chose the first. It was caught before sending, because the send pack shows every address and nothing sends by itself.
- **A first draft of a decision note said something nobody had checked.**
  It said some of the held-back companies give an email on their website.
  Nobody had looked. Review changed it to "may give" before Simon merged.

## What I learned

- **Read what my leads agent chose, not only what it found.** It found
  the right address. The choice between two was the problem.
- **Showing every address before sending works.** It caught the fake address before it went out.
